## Own the decision, evidence and counterevidence

Frame the investment question before collecting feature lists. Distinguish users, buyers, organizational gatekeepers and reachable segments. Separate customer observations, product tests, vendor claims, analyst inference and unanswered questions. Recommend against building when evidence favors a cheaper alternative or no change.

Use current primary sources for competitor coverage, capture mechanisms, privacy, pricing and distribution claims. Record source URL, access date, stated update/version, platform, client and relevant plan. Keep contradictory claims side by side; a vendor claim is not firsthand validation. Do not infer a market gap from one unsupported platform claim or invent market size, customers or interviews.

## Diagnose adoption as a funnel

Distinguish exposure/discovery, listing visit, install, onboarding completion, first useful outcome and repeat use. Define numerator, denominator and comparable period before reporting a rate. Installation and removal events do not establish active users, retention, churn or causation. Reconcile duplicate exports, missing dimensions, date windows and release exposure before drawing conclusions.

Inspect existing analytics and authorized records before proposing new collection. Report which funnel transitions cannot be measured. Do not add telemetry, transmit meeting content, contact users, spend money or activate services merely to answer a research question. Apply explicit authorization already supplied for those actions.

## Research and decisions

Use neutral questions about recent actual behavior before showing concepts. Record recruitment source, eligibility, organizational independence, refusals and sampling limits. A small purposive sample reveals jobs and failure modes; it does not estimate prevalence, conversion or willingness-to-pay distributions. Synthetic scenarios and AI personas are research preparation, not participant evidence.

Compare the current product and built-in substitutes with proposed expansion. Predeclare evidence that would advance, narrow or stop the proposal; distinguish a low-cost feasibility experiment from approval to build a supported product. Engineering owns technical feasibility, QA/security own verification and data boundaries, and FinOps owns verified service economics. Do not create a circular gate requiring a completed prototype before permitting an experiment to establish feasibility.

Deliver a concise evidence ledger, prioritized hypotheses, smallest next research actions and a decision brief with limitations and disconfirming evidence. Keep project-specific priorities and findings in project records rather than this portable skill.
