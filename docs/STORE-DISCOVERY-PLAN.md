# Chrome and Edge Store discovery plan

Research refreshed October 10, 2026. This is a release checklist, not evidence
that either Store has accepted or published the 5.4 candidate.

## What the stores say affects discovery

- Chrome says the title should be concise, unique, and describe the core
  function. Its 132-character summary is the main text shown in search and
  category results. The description should use important feature terms in
  context, not keyword lists. Chrome ranking also considers ratings and usage,
  including downloads compared with uninstalls.
- Chrome recommends all five 1280 x 800 screenshots, current real product UI,
  consistent branding, restrained text, and visual aids that explain the core
  workflow. A 440 x 280 promo tile can appear in search and category surfaces.
- Edge states that search ranking uses item characteristics, including name,
  description relevance, screenshots, publisher information, popularity, and
  user experience. Ratings and average rating influence popularity ordering.
- Both stores therefore require two parallel tracks: accurate searchable
  metadata and legitimate adoption evidence. Copy alone cannot overcome six
  users and no ratings.

Primary guidance:

- Chrome: https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/best-listing
- Chrome keyword-spam policy: https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/policies
- Edge ranking and curation: https://learn.microsoft.com/microsoft-edge/extensions/publish/add-ons-curation
- Edge listing quality: https://learn.microsoft.com/microsoft-edge/extensions/developer-guide/best-practices

## Competitive search pattern

Scribbl and Tactiq lead with the category phrase **AI note taker**, immediately
name Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams, and place live transcription in
the first sentence. Their listings also have Featured status, tens of thousands
to a million users, thousands of ratings, many listing languages, and polished
visuals. Those popularity signals cannot be copied. The reusable lesson is to
name the buyer problem before explaining the architecture.

Better CaptionKeep must not describe itself as an automatic cloud note taker.
Its truthful search vocabulary is:

- Primary: live meeting transcript; meeting captions; Microsoft Teams;
  Google Meet; Zoom Web.
- Differentiator: On the Fly AI research; cited evidence; source-linked;
  local transcript; no meeting bot.
- Enterprise proof: managed browser policy; customer-owned Microsoft 365 and
  AI; explicit meeting controls; reviewed handoff.

Use each phrase naturally where it explains a real capability. Do not append
keyword lists, repeat a phrase more than needed, name competitors in metadata,
or claim ratings, leadership, certification, or Store endorsement.

## Release metadata

**Name**

Better CaptionKeep — Live Meeting Transcript

**Summary**

Local live meeting transcripts for Teams, Google Meet, and Zoom, with
source-linked On the Fly AI research and cited evidence.

**Category**

Workflow & Planning

The first description paragraph must repeat the user story in natural prose:
capture displayed captions locally, select an exact claim during the meeting,
research it through the customer's assistant, inspect cited evidence, and copy
an editable reply. Follow it with privacy and enterprise controls—not a long
architecture preamble.

## Five-image story

1. **One side panel. Three meeting platforms.** Real Live Workspace UI and
   Teams, Google Meet, and Zoom Web support.
2. **Nothing starts until you choose it.** The visible caption, attendee, and
   Teams transcript controls with their status states.
3. **Research the exact words—On the Fly.** A highlighted caption excerpt and
   the reviewed outbound-evidence preview.
4. **Answers that keep their sources.** A cited Research Card, source caption
   identifier, and editable live-chat reply boundary.
5. **Your browser. Your tenant. Your assistant.** Local storage, managed policy,
   customer-owned Microsoft 365, and customer-owned AI/connector controls.

Every screenshot must use synthetic meeting content, actual current UI, a
single visual system, large readable headings, and no more than one main claim.

## Search and adoption verification

Before submission, record dated results for these unauthenticated searches in
both stores: `Better CaptionKeep`, `live meeting transcript`, `meeting captions
Teams`, `Google Meet transcript local`, `Zoom live transcript extension`, and
`meeting AI research cited evidence`. Search indexing can lag publication, so
repeat the same check after the update becomes public.

Run a small managed pilot and ask only real users for honest reviews after they
have completed the workflow. Track listing impressions, installs, successful
first capture, seven-day retention, uninstalls, and review count. Never seed or
purchase reviews.
